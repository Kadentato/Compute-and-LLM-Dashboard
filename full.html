Token — the basic unit of LLM work, roughly three-quarters of a word  ·  Open weight — the model can be downloaded and run by anyone  ·  Gateway — a routing service apps use to reach many models through one API.

Scope and limitations

This is gateway traffic: developer tools, agents, and apps routing through OpenRouter and Vercel AI Gateway. It is a proxy, not the market share of open models overall.

Full scope and limitations

Consumer chat — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude.ai, Grok — never touches a gateway, and no lab publishes token counts. No public source measures "all tokens in the world"; this page doesn't pretend to.

The two gateways disagree because they see different customers: OpenRouter skews cost-sensitive and hobbyist, Vercel skews production apps. The spread between them is a finding, not an error — which is why there is no blended average.

Tokens through each gateway's aggregated "other" bucket and unidentifiable (cloaked pre-release) models are excluded from both lines — the gray band in the composition charts below shows how much that is.

The Vercel line before May 2026 is a floor, not a measurement: Vercel only names each day's top models, and until DeepSeek V4 broke into that list in late April 2026, open-weight usage sat inside the unclassifiable "Other" bucket.

Volume versus money

Vercel: open-weight share of tokens vs share of spend

Same models, two ledgers — the share of Vercel's tokens that open-weight models serve (the work done) against the share of Vercel's spend they receive (the dollars paid). The shaded gap is the economics of open weights: far cheaper per token, they carry most of the work for a fraction of the money. OpenRouter publishes no spend data, so this panel is Vercel only.

The absolute market behind every share on this page: total tokens OpenRouter routes per day. A share is only as meaningful as the volume underneath it — this is that volume. Vercel publishes no absolute numbers, which is why its panels are shares only.

      Beyond the gateways

      The gateways only see developer traffic. These two panels are the closest automatic signals for everything else — and each is labelled with exactly what it does and does not measure.

      Consumer signal

      Generative AI services by DNS popularity — rank order only, no volumes

      Daily rank of consumer-facing AI services by DNS traffic on Cloudflare's 1.1.1.1 resolver, sampled weekly. A rank says who is ahead, never by how much. Consumer-facing services only — developer platforms such as Hugging Face are excluded (see the open-ecosystem panel instead). Lines are every service that has ranked top-four at any point, past leaders like Character.AI included. Where a service briefly fell outside the day's published list or below the chart's floor, the line is bridged by interpolation or pinned to the bottom edge — readability assumptions; the tooltip and CSV keep the exact ranks.
